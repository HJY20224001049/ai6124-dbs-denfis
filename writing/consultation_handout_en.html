01What we built

Thirteen causal technical features, standardised on training data only, feed an ECM clustering stage that grows and merges rules online. Gaussian activations over those rules produce a first-order Takagi–Sugeno prediction, with consequents estimated by membership-weighted recursive least squares. Two variants are reported: frozen, fitted once on the training window, and online, which predicts first and then updates on the realised label.

Evaluation uses nine expanding walk-forward folds covering 2017–2025. Hyper-parameters are chosen on the preceding validation year only. The 2026 holdout is untouched and reserved for the scheduled freeze.

03The gap is not a test-set accident

On the validation years, before any test data is seen, DENFIS is already behind XGBoost in seven of nine folds. The shortfall is a property of the model on this data, not an unlucky test period. That is why we stopped trying to fix it by re-running the final evaluation.

05What the literature suggests, and what it does not

Published improvements to this model family are almost always compared against other fuzzy models, such as eTS, SAFIS, PANFIS or FLEXFIS, and rarely against a tuned gradient-boosting baseline. We could not find a study reporting an evolving fuzzy system beating a well-tuned GBM on daily equity returns.

The mechanisms the literature credits (type-2 fuzzy sets, participatory learning, kernel RLS, concept-drift detection, rule pruning, online bagging) mostly add capacity or adaptation. That is the opposite of what our diagnostics point to: on this data every capacity increase has made validation error worse.

06What we deliberately did not do

We did not select parameters, features, blend weights or folds using test-year performance. Six exploratory directions could each have been reported as a win by quoting the best fold or the best parameter point. Each is reported above with the grid-wide result that contradicts it. A fresh run reproduces the committed results to about 1e-13.

The three checks we brought

A

Target definition. Our label is ln(Pt+1/Pt), a price difference rather than a price level (src/features.py:87). A pilot that predicts the price level literally shows that the best model can only learn P̂ ≈ Pt: price RMSE improves by just 0.07%, which is −0.20% back in return space; XGBoost cannot extrapolate a price level, predicting a mean of 25.27 against an actual 31.57 in 2024.

B

Implementation. On a synthetic target with a known signal (y = 0.25·ret₀ + N(0, 0.012), same folds), the same pipeline beats no prediction by 2.45% for DENFIS, 2.10% for XGBoost and 1.48% for LSTM. The pipeline does learn a real signal; the DBS daily signal sits below the estimation noise.

C

Scope. Against the no-prediction benchmark all four models are worse: zero-forecast RMSE 0.011975, XGBoost 0.012009 (−0.28%), LSTM 0.012043 (−0.57%), DENFIS frozen 0.012066 (−0.76%). The shortfall is not specific to DENFIS.
