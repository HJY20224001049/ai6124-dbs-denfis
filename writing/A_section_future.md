## 9. Future work

More independent evaluation data would strengthen the assessment of the observed Sharpe differences. A panel of SGX banks, or a collection of comparable markets, would extend the evidence beyond nine years of a single stock. The central question would remain whether the forecasting approach produces a consistent advantage across independent observations. Such an extension would provide a broader basis for interpreting differences between the model strategies and their benchmark. The present single-stock record does not resolve those Sharpe differences reliably. Additional independent data would therefore address the evidential limitation directly, rather than treating the current ranking as a settled conclusion about the relative quality of the methods.

A second direction is to consider a target for which local rules have a clearer structural role. Realised volatility and returns measured over several days are the two alternatives proposed here. The motivation is to examine whether the relationship being learned is better matched to the model's local representation. This changes the question from predicting the next daily return to modelling a quantity for which local conditions may be more informative. Such targets would require their own evaluation; their suitability cannot be established from the present results. They offer a focused continuation of the same modelling approach, while keeping the rationale tied to what local rules are intended to represent.

A third direction is a more principled treatment of model complexity. The present implementation limits the rule base to two hundred rules, but that numerical ceiling does not express the predictive value of retaining an additional rule. An alternative is to assign each rule a cost measured in validation-error units. A proposed increase in complexity would then need to deliver enough validation improvement to justify that cost. This would connect the size of the rule base directly to the criterion used to assess its predictions. The resulting question is how much additional local structure is worth keeping, rather than whether the current rule count has merely reached a fixed implementation limit.

Finally, online adaptation should be examined over longer streams. Each current fold supplies one year of test observations, which gives the adapting model a limited period in which to recover the variance introduced by its updates. Extending the stream beyond one test year per fold would allow the balance between adaptation and update variability to be assessed over a longer interval. The relevant question is whether the accumulated benefit of updating can compensate for that additional variance. This direction concerns the duration available to the existing adaptation mechanism, rather than a claim that adaptation must improve performance. Longer streams would provide the setting needed to investigate that question more directly.
